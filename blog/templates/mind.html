On Doing Less, Better

I cut my to-do list in half for a month. The work that survived was the work that mattered.

17 Sep 2026·1 min read·Mind & Meaning / Habits

For most of this year my to-do list was a trophy cabinet. Forty items on a good day, sixty on a bad one, and a quiet pride in how full it looked. The list was busy, so I assumed I was too.

In August I tried something that felt irresponsible. Every morning, before starting, I cut the list in half. Not by working faster. By deciding which half didn't deserve to exist.

The first week was uncomfortable. Some of what I dropped came back, louder. But most of it never came back at all, and that was the part I couldn't stop thinking about.

“

Most of what fills a day could be left undone, and nothing would fall.

Doing less isn't the same as being lazy. The work that survived the cut got more of me: more hours, more attention, and the kind of thinking you can only do when you aren't already looking at what's next.

What I kept

Three rules survived the month. The list gets written the night before, never in the morning. Anything that sits on it for three days either gets done today or gets deleted. And one thing a day is allowed to be the whole day.

I don't know yet if this lasts. Habits look permanent right up until they aren't. But for now the list is short, the work is better, and I go to sleep like someone who finished something.
